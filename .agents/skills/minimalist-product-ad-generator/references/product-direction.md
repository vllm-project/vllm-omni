# Product anchors, typography, and beats

## Narrative choices

- Launch: negative space → hero product → material detail → real mechanism action
  → supporting variants → stable product and copy.
- Feature touch: stillness → interaction trigger → visible mechanism → detail
  magnification → useful result → closing.
- Color family: main variant alone → supporting colors enter → ordered relation
  → unified material finish → full set and copy.

## Independent anchor briefs

| Anchor | What it locks | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Main variant, recognizable silhouette, attractive opening view | Match supplied body color and product structure |
| Detail | Material, reflection, texture, visible mechanism | No invented ports, controls, or functionality |
| Closing | Product position, negative space, typography treatment | Real copy, readable contrast, no panel/grid layout |

Each is a standalone full-frame image with the project ratio. Reference roles
protect appearance and layout; they do not replace the storyboard or require three
sequential static-image segments. Simplify the number of anchors if the backend
cannot bind them all, and update the prompt labels accordingly.

## Typography

Use one readable line at a time, with a typeface appropriate to the brand. A clean
semibold sans-serif is a useful default, not a requirement to copy a specific brand.
For a two-part color treatment, use dark text on a light field or light text on a
dark field, then emphasize the second part with a specific real product color.
Check contrast rather than blindly coloring text like the product.

The first phrase may enter gently; the second follows while the first shifts
slightly within the same line. Do not accidentally turn this into stacked lines.
Use actual copy in the prompt and spell out its timing. Avoid arrows, separators,
or placeholder marks that the model may reproduce. For a 10-second ad, one middle
copy moment and one stable closing line are often enough. Omit copy when requested.

## Beat table

| Time range | Shot purpose | Visual lead and camera | Variant state | Exact copy | Text color/motion | Handoff | Rhythm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use one main action per beat. For 5/10/15-second films, roughly 3–4/5–7/6–9 beats
are planning suggestions, not required cuts. Record the ending product position
and motion vector so the next beat can inherit them. Ensure the closing line has
time to be read rather than using every second for motion.

If replacing music, keep useful SFX unless replacement of the whole track was
requested. Use a continuous excerpt with a clear entry, stable middle, and suitable
ending. A short tail fade may resolve a click; do not hide an incomplete phrase
behind a long fade. Confirm the entire result remains audible.
