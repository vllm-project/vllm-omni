# Paper staging and prompt building blocks

## Materials and depth

Use layered matte cardboard, visible thickness, fibers, folds, seams, tabs, brads,
cut edges, and physical shadows. Separate foreground, mechanism, background, and
far background with spacing. Roughly 4–7 layers often suffice; remove layers that
hide the explanation. Place reading-heavy labels on stable planes. Use miniature
macro photography and moderate depth of field without blurring necessary facts.

| Plane | Useful content | Motion | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Foreground | Paper leaves, frame, curtain, props | Small parallax or hinged reveal | Depth without concealing the mechanism |
| Midground | Knowledge model, host, arrow/cards | Pull-tab, slider, joint, rotating disc | Main cause and effect |
| Background | Diorama set or schematic layers | Slow paper rail shift | Context and hierarchy |
| Far plane | Paper sky/board | Restrained change | Stable visual support |

## Prompt ingredients

Image: topic and core object → miniature diorama → layered paper construction →
specific material edges → physical light/shadows → clear depth → necessary labels.
One frame should communicate one main idea, not the entire lesson at once.

Image series: repeat the style/identity anchors, then state what changes and why
that state explains the next knowledge step. Keep the same ratio and light direction.

Short animation: preserve the supplied image, make a small paper mechanism move,
include tiny pauses/rebounds, and select one restrained camera move. Small puppet
gestures and background rails can provide life without overwhelming the model.

## Camera and transitions

Use slow push-in, lateral parallax, static demonstration, macro detail, or a slight
top-down cross-section view. A full orbit requires a set built for that view.
Transitions may use a page flip, pop-up unfold, pull-tab, paper cloud, circular
mask, cut-paper door, layer separation, confetti, or sticker reveal. Avoid liquid
morphing, neon glitches, and glass shatter unless explicitly requested as contrast.

## Sound and exclusions

Use a few motion-specific accents: paper flip, scissors, cardboard slide, joint
tap, tape peel, or box opening. A score, when appropriate, can use light percussion,
plucked instruments, or topic-specific instrumentation. Do not infer narration
merely because the video teaches something.

Keep constraints concise and relevant: no glossy plastic, smooth CG morphs, metal
surfaces replacing paper, missing thickness/shadows, or frantic camera movement.
Include them in supported prompt fields; do not invent a negative-prompt API field.
